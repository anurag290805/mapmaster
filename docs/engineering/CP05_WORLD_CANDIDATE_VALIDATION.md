# CP05 world candidate validation — 2026-11-01

## Scope and decision

This is a development-only interaction investigation, not geographic approval or permission to replace the live World Edition. `pilot/world/` loads `candidate/world.svg` and its manifest, reuses `pilot/core.mjs` for selection, generation ownership and viewport bounds, and leaves the eight-country pilot and live gameplay intact. Build configuration excludes both `pilot/` and `candidate/` from production. No remote geography service is used. Canonical IDs remain independent of the provisional Natural Earth de facto boundary view; that view represents territorial control, not a sovereignty determination. Public release requires applicable Indian and other distribution requirements to be checked.

## Inventory and geographic limitations

The versioned manifest lists 194 canonical country groups with source feature associations and 64 noninteractive source-only context features. Manifest/SVG tests check distinct IDs, each canonical association and every multipart count; the registry validator checks ledger structure, not the truth of a boundary. The coverage report retains eight independently checked pilot identities and 186 code-corroborated identities, alongside the older 165 reconciled, 22 policy-dependent and seven ambiguous statuses. None is automatically promoted to approved live geography. The exact records, aliases, identities, source IDs, policy flags and blockers are in `CP05_WORLD_COVERAGE.json` and `candidate/world-manifest.json`.

Natural Earth Admin-0 Countries 10m version 5.1.1 archive SHA-256 is `ce1ac7036499a0edd641fbc093cd209a98f96a49d2eca8480aaacad35138a7f6`. Its [terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/) state that data are public domain and may be used commercially without required attribution. The source had one invalid shape: Egypt's self-intersecting ring. GEOS `make_valid` produced eight valid parts with negligible planar area change and no positive-area overlap with the inspected 10m Israel, Libya or Sudan shapes. All 194 candidate geometries passed validity after eight-decimal rounding and the 194-target overlap scan found no positive-area intersections above 1e-10 square degrees. These are planar diagnostics, not proof of complete geographic correctness; Egypt's repair still needs independent geographic review.

Canada has 412 mapped polygon parts, Indonesia 264, Maldives 176, Fiji 44, Taiwan eight and Vatican City one. Existing cross-scale audit exposes seven unresolved 10m/50m discrepancies: Seychelles, Maldives, Kiribati, Marshall Islands, Tuvalu, Monaco and Vatican City. Vatican 50m and 10m polygons have disjoint bounds, and 50m Maldives has two parts versus 176 at 10m. The chooser provides an interaction fallback, but it does not verify geographic placement or make every island directly clickable. Watchlist entries remain Armenia, Azerbaijan, China, Cyprus, Egypt, Georgia, India, Iran, Iraq, Israel, Morocco, Pakistan, Palestine, Russia, Serbia, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Taiwan, Turkey and Ukraine; the watchlist is conservative and not exhaustive. Taiwan's UN code evidence is not a sovereignty determination. Inherited asset licensing also remains a release concern.

## Harness interaction and evidence

Map groups are validated against the 194 manifest IDs and part counts before installation. The map exposes generic numbered accessible target names to avoid disclosing quiz answers; named chooser options provide a non-pointer path. Mouse and touch use native SVG hit testing and pointer capture; keyboard Enter/Space selects a focused target. The view has bounded zoom, drag pan, directional pan, reset and chooser-based focus. Review suppresses scoring; new session and reload invalidate ownership. The harness does not establish production accessibility or the historical root cause of I-15.

Browser tests use actual Playwright mouse, keyboard, chooser and touch-emulation actions. Synthetic keyboard events are used only to probe stale detached handlers and are not counted as hit testing. No physical device or comprehensive assistive-technology testing has been performed. Fine-grained results and measurements follow in the verification record.

## Size and performance investigation

The SVG is 14,024,221 bytes locally. `gzip -c -n -9 candidate/world.svg` yielded 5,370,679 bytes; this is an offline compression measurement, not evidence that a server actually sends gzip. Whole-world replacement with Natural Earth's smaller 50m layer is unsafe without separate geographic review because of the seven discrepancies above. Simplifying or mixing resolution could reduce size, but has not been validated across all 194 geometries and must not be assumed safe. No altered geometry was promoted in this investigation.

The detailed local Brave samples (readiness, selection, viewport actions and JS heap after repeated loads) are recorded in `CP05_VERIFICATION.md`. Heap samples measure V8-reported JS heap after requested garbage collection, not native SVG DOM/rendering allocations, GPU usage or total browser memory; a few reloads cannot establish long-run leak freedom. Timings are local single-run Playwright observations, not production network or physical-mobile benchmarks.

### Current interaction coverage

In a development Brave run, all 194 canonical targets were completed through browser controls: one correct actual mouse hit (Afghanistan), one keyboard Enter (Albania), and 192 chooser submissions. Every question also received one deliberately incorrect country selection and rejected it without increasing the correct score. This demonstrates 194/194 supported selection paths for the diagnostic harness, not geographic verification and not 194 pointer-reachable targets. The chooser's 192 correct entries include Vatican City and the other small-country exceptions. Two different Canada polygon components were actually clicked: the first scored Canada and the second was correctly rejected once the current question changed. Brazil was also actually clicked as a wrong target. The touch-emulation run tapped Afghanistan at a hit-tested integer pixel on a 390px viewport; no physical device was used. The map has generic accessible target labels, and the chooser supplies named alternatives. Keyboard, pointer focus, zoom, pan buttons, reset, selected-country focus, review scoring suppression and small-viewport layout were exercised. Tiny targets are not claimed to be reliably mouse- or touch-reachable at world scale.

The first touch attempt selected Tajikistan: the initial test derived a fractional coordinate inside Afghanistan, while the tap rounded to an adjacent pixel. The point-finding test now validates the actual integer coordinate with `elementFromPoint` before issuing Playwright's touch action; the rerun passed. This illustrates why geometric point containment alone is not proof of touch hit testing.

A held first map response was released after a later map became active; the later document remained installed. A further held response was interrupted by navigation, and the separate pilot loaded. Tests also fired synthetic events on detached old nodes, exercised review suppression and session reset, and reloaded after repeated viewport actions. The earlier eight-country pilot includes a guard-removal control demonstrating the generation-guard race; no such guard-removal claim is made for this new harness. Repeated replacement's V8 heap samples and broader limitations appear below.

A local targeted run measured 828 ms from navigation to ready, 28 ms for a real mouse selection plus assertion, 721 ms initial ready on a 390px viewport, 696 ms for a mobile-viewport reload, and 654 ms for six Playwright zoom/pan button actions. A separate four-sample CDP V8 heap experiment after requested garbage collections measured 2,357,704 / 2,577,272 / 2,836,140 / 2,854,440 bytes over three reloads. The increase warrants longer profiling; these numbers exclude native SVG, rendering and browser memory and cannot establish leak freedom. Machine load, Playwright overhead and warm local filesystem affect all samples.
